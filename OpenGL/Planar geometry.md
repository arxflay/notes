**Square units (surface)** - the size of each side of a square; for example, a 5x5cm square contains 25 squares (each has a side length of 1cm), or in other words, its surface is $25cm^2$

The volume of a box is $V = h*l*w$, where $h$ is the height, $l$ is the length, and $w$ is the width

**area of a triangle** = $\dfrac{b * h}{2}$ where b is the base and h is the shortest height to the base

**Circle circumference** is the line around a circle (defining the whole circle). It is computed via $2\pi r = C$ (r is repeated $2 \pi$ to form a circle)

**Arc length**: Computed via $S = \theta  r$, where $\theta$ is an angle in radians. The formula is derived from the ratio of the arc length and the ratio of rotation, $S/C = \theta / 2\pi$, $S/2\pi r= \theta / 2\pi$, $S = \theta  r$, proved by substituting S = C; then $\theta$ must be $2\pi$, thus $1 = 1$. If the angle is not in radians, then the formula contains a conversion from degrees to radians $S = \dfrac{\theta \pi r}{180}$

**Circle area**: the formula is $A = \dfrac{1}{2} 2\pi r^2 = \pi r^2$, which can be imagined as $r^2$ half of the square repeated $\pi$ times.

**A circle sector** is a closed region formed inside a circle from the center and two points on the circle (disk). Typical types are a half-disk ($180 \textdegree$), quadrant ($90 \textdegree$), and sextant ($60\textdegree$)
The area of a circle sector is derived from the formula for the area of a circle, $A = \dfrac{\theta r^2}{2}$ (proved by picking $\theta$ equal to $2 \pi$)

**The volume of a cylinder** is $V=\pi r^2h$ (imagine it as the area of a circle expanded by height $h$)

## Chord intersection theorem

^00d4f6

If two chords intersect and divide each chord into two lengths: a and b for the first chord and c and d for the second chord, then $ab = cd$, e.g., the areas of the two rectangles formed by the divided chords are identical. The reason is that intersecting chords form 2 triangles at the point of intersection. Each triangle is a similar triangle because both angles are on the same arc [[Angles#^8de587 |Subtended angle theorem]]. If we construct two triangles and compare them side by side, then they will form a parallelogram
